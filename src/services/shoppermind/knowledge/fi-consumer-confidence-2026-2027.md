# Finland consumer confidence — 2026–2027 baseline scenario

## Base case (2026)

A reasonable base case is that Finland's consumer confidence stays negative through
much of 2026 but improves modestly later in the year, rather than snapping back to
normal quickly. Official and macro sources point to gradual economic recovery in
2026, but from a weak starting point marked by unemployment concerns and subdued
sentiment.

Trading Economics' model projects Finland's consumer confidence at roughly **-7.0 on
average in 2026**, after expecting about **-11.0 by end of the current quarter**.
That implies improvement from weak early-2026 readings but still leaves confidence
below the long-term average of **-2.8** — stabilisation first, partial recovery
later.

## Why it may improve

- European Commission expects Finland's economy to return to growth in 2026 at
  about **+1.0% GDP**.
- Bank of Finland says private consumption should start rising in 2026 as real
  earnings improve, market interest payments ease, and the labour market gradually
  strengthens.
- Mortgage pressure easing and employment stabilising typically support household
  sentiment with a lag.

## What holds it back

- Unemployment remains elevated.
- Household views of the broader economy stayed very weak in January/February
  releases.
- Statistics Finland's latest kbar publications show fear of unemployment, weak
  views of the national economy, and less favourable attitudes toward borrowing
  and major purchases.

## Qualitative forecast

| Period  | Expected state |
|---------|----------------|
| Q2 2026 | Still weak, around early-2026 depressed levels |
| Q3 2026 | Possible mild improvement if rates, wages, labour stabilise |
| Q4 2026 | Better than Q1 but likely still below long-run average unless labour clearly improves |

**Working range for late 2026:** negative but gradually improving, **around -9 to -6
by late 2026**, with downside risk if unemployment worsens.

## 2027 view

A reasonable 2027 expectation is **material improvement from 2026** but confidence
likely remains **near or slightly below normal** rather than strongly optimistic.
Trading Economics' long-term projection is about **-2.0 in 2027** — close to
Finland's historical average of -2.8 and a clear improvement from 2026.

## How ShopperMind should use this

- When the kbar CCI_A1 StatFin signal crosses above -9 for two consecutive months,
  treat it as **confirming** this baseline (state → STABLE / ACCELERATING).
- When kbar readings stay below -11 into Q3 2026, flag as **downside deviation** —
  mortgage pressure or labour deterioration may be worse than expected.
- Narrative briefings should frame CCI changes against this baseline range, not
  against zero, to avoid over-reacting to monthly noise.
- For retail tenancy signals (store closures, bankruptcies) in H1 2026, treat as
  consistent with the baseline rather than regime-change evidence — the baseline
  already prices in weak consumption.
